# D-18 — Content engine

**Status: plan. Nothing published.**

The research series (D-16) is finite. This is what keeps going after it.

## The rule that makes it worth reading

**Every post traces to a citation in `docs/research/focus-science.md`.** No post ships with a claim
that file doesn't support. Where the evidence is thin, the post says so — "this is the obvious rule
and it's untested" is a sentence readers trust, and it costs nothing to be honest about.

## Sources of posts, in order of value

1. **Design decisions with a reason behind them.** `docs/DECISIONS.md` is already a content backlog.
   "Why breaks auto-start and focus blocks don't" is a real post with a real argument, and writing it
   costs almost nothing because the reasoning is already recorded.
2. **Build-in-public on the calibration problem.** The estimate-vs-actual idea is genuinely
   uncommon; the honest uncertainty about whether people will *use* it is interesting in itself.
3. **Answers to questions asked in communities.** Written once, linkable forever.
4. **What the data says, once there is any.** Not before ~100 users/day — below that nothing
   separates signal from noise, and posting a chart of 40 sessions would be exactly the
   overclaiming this whole approach avoids.

## Cadence

Whatever survives 5–10 hours a week alongside building. One good post a month beats four rushed
ones, and a series that stops after three posts is worse than one that was always monthly.

## Things not to write

- Listicles of productivity tips. Every competitor has them, they rank badly now, and they say
  nothing only Delva could say.
- Anything claiming focus sounds improve concentration. The evidence is mixed and the guardrail is
  absolute.
- Anything positioning ADHD as a deficit that a timer fixes.
- Comparison posts against named competitors. They read as insecure and they date badly.
